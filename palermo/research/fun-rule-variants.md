# Fun-oriented rule toggles for a six-seat AI Palermo

Research note, 2026-11-14. New material only; it builds on `palermo-rules.md`, `roles-catalog.md` and `how-games-go.md` and does not repeat their role-by-role analysis. Method: web search plus page fetches. Everything is paraphrased; the few quotes are under 15 words. "Unverified" marks a claim seen only in a search snippet, a secondary summary, or my own inference. The fit column is my reasoning from six-seat arithmetic (two killers, plurality vote, quiet night 1), not a citation.

Blocked or thin, stated up front: MafiaScum wiki and forum (403, so the named open setups Vengeful, White Flag, Lovers, Fire and Ice were not read; only a search snippet on "Vengeful" and "Matrix 6"), BoardGameGeek (not reached), Town of Salem fandom wiki (not fetched; only the Wikipedia article and third-party guides), Davidoff's original rules (no primary text found), Mafia Universe (no rules page found), Blood on the Clocktower's `design-philosophy` page (404).

## (a) Summary

1. The strongest cross-source finding is that elimination is the genre's main complaint, and the best fix is to keep the dead involved (BotC dead voice and one vote, ONUW no elimination, Resistance none).
2. BotC's designer says dead votes are a balance tool: without them each town death adds to evil's voting share and the game snowballs.
3. Small tables (5-6) end too fast: one town death near parity and the game is over, with little shared history for suspense (werewolv.es). Fixes proposed there: spare lives, a fake-able role for wolves, a parity-neutral helper, no night-1 kill.
4. Day 1 is near-random everywhere; the designer-side fixes are information (night-zero seer view, UW guidance), a second chance after a mislynch (spare life, hunter), or a structured accusation round.
5. Quiet or "vanilla" roles feel they have nothing to do (Bumbling Through Dungeons); giving every seat something to say (trusted pair, partial information, a last shot) is the recurring remedy.
6. Early reveal of a detective is a known mistake; unreliable or partial information keeps the detective from ending the game (BotC drunk/poisoned; werewolv.es on strong seers at low counts).
7. Drama mechanics with the best fit at six: last will, defence speech before the vote, hunter/vengeful last shot, ghost vote. Worst fit: anything that removes a town seat outside the vote (as in the earlier notes).
8. Spectator evidence is thin: no study found on dramatic irony. Soft evidence says visible reasoning and readable failure drive enjoyment (AI Mafia site, Among Us commentary).
9. Designer principle worth borrowing: make the opposing team's powers strong too, rather than +1/-1 tuning (Medway). For us that means giving killers real bluffing material.
10. Treat balance as a means to fun, not a goal (minifiniti essay); our six-seat table is already town-hostile on paper, so watchability beats a 50/50 win rate.

## (b) Candidate rule toggles

Fit key: good / ok / poor for a six-seat AI table.

| # | Toggle | What it changes | Fun problem solved | Source | Fit at 6 seats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ghost vote** (dead players keep exactly one vote for the rest of the game, spent on any later day) | Eliminated seats still decide votes; dead players may also keep talking | Early elimination = nothing to do; snowball toward killers | BotC rules and Medway's balance post: https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation , https://bloodontheclocktower.com/blogs/news/behind-the-curtain-7-balance | **good**: at 6 seats most games have 2-3 dead by the end, so ghost votes can outnumber living ones and make the last day dramatic; watch that dead killers' votes do not just grief (they vote with their team, which spectators will enjoy) |
| 2 | **Dead talk** (dead players keep speaking in day discussion, without ability or nomination) | Dead remain in conversation | Same; also gives spectators more voices | BotC (dead still talk): https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation ; contrast Plotkin/Greek: silence | **ok**: more tokens and slower games; a dead killer who talks can reveal too much, so pair with toggle 1 or with a one-line cap |
| 3 | **Dead-only chat** (a "graveyard" the living cannot see, visible to viewers) | Ghosts converse privately, spectators watch both | Dead seats idle; audience gets commentary | Live Action Mafia graveyard forum (secondary: https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Mafia_%28party_game%29 , unverified details) | **ok**: great for viewers and comedy, but with only 2-3 dead it is a thin room; risk of agents leaking info to the living through behaviour, so keep it display-only |
| 4 | **Last will** (each player may write a short will, published at dawn if killed at night or on a vote) | Night victims leave one note; killers can plant fake wills | Night victims contribute nothing; no clues from deaths | Town of Salem wills revealed after death (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_Salem); a forum variant where a will publishes on death (Bulbagarden, search snippet: https://bulbagarden.net/threads/mafia-v-1.140483/latest , unverified) | **good**: cheap, funny, gives day 2 a topic; a will written in advance must be vague or it is a self-reveal; consider writing it at death rather than ahead |

| 6 | **Accusation trials, up to N per day** (ToS-style: any player may put someone on trial; trial ends with a guilty/innocent verdict; tie = innocent) | Replaces a single day vote with sequential trials, possibly several | One-shot vote with a plurality coin flip | ToS (tie = innocent, guilty ends the day; search snippets, unverified: https://town-of-salem.fandom.com/wiki/Trial_System) | **poor**: with two killers at six, a tie-means-innocent trial gives killers a free pass every day; the earlier notes already show a no-lynch is a killer win |

| 8 | **Spare life ("doubles")** (one or two players hold two lives; a surviving execution is announced publicly) | An execution can fail; the table learns "this person had a spare life" | Day 1 coin flip; games ending in two days | werewolv.es six-player Doubles variant, with bot-simulated 61-63 percent wolf win rate under normal play (a tool-generated estimate, not human data): https://werewolv.es/small-setups/playing-werewolf-mafia-with-six-players | **ok**: adds days and a free information event; but it is a new role the officer cannot read and the killers may exploit; try as a single "enduring" citizen |
| 9 | **Parity-neutral accomplice** (one killer plus an accomplice who knows the killer, is unknown to them, and counts as town for parity) | Third evil voice without a second kill | A second killer is too strong at 6; wolves with no one to deceive | werewolv.es Bloodmoon Cult idea (explicitly untested: https://werewolv.es/small-setups/why-low-player-count-werewolf-stops-feeling-like-werewolf ); engine already has `accomplice` flag | **ok**: playable but replaces the shown/hidden killer pair, so it is a different game; interesting for a variant night |
| 10 | **Fake-able role card for killers** (each killer also holds a town role card, e.g. a killer can bluff as officer or doctor with cover) | Makes claims arguable instead of proof | Role claims treated as proof; officer result is trusted too fast | werewolv.es ("giving wolves village roles"): https://werewolv.es/small-setups/why-low-player-count-werewolf-stops-feeling-like-werewolf | **poor**: confusing to implement; the engine already lets killers claim anything, so the gain is mostly about how much agents believe a claim |

## (c) Design principles for fun, with sources

1. **Death should not mean leaving.** BotC keeps dead players talking, voting once and hoping to influence the end, and Medway says dead votes counteract a runaway-leader effect (https://bloodontheclocktower.com/blogs/news/behind-the-curtain-7-balance). The Resistance has no elimination at all (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_(game)); ONUW compresses to one night so nobody sits out (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Night_Ultimate_Werewolf). The minifiniti essay calls the alternative "mental elimination": lose options, not the seat.
2. **Elimination has defenders too.** The League of Gamemakers piece argues the risk of elimination creates the tension that drives talk and that watching afterward can still be engaging (https://leagueofgamemakers.com/the-problems-with-werewolf). Keep eliminations, soften their cost.
3. **Information is the cure for "I'm not a werewolf" loops.** Tao of Gaming finds the base game dull because of repeated denials; BotC gives each seat role-based information to reason with (https://bloodontheclocktower.com/blogs/news/behind-the-curtain-4-werewolf-clocktower-how-they-are-different-how-they-are-the-same ; https://20thcenturygames.substack.com/p/mafia-werewolf).
4. **Evil needs material to bluff with.** BotC advises evil to lie broadly and attack the early links in the good team's logic chain (https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Player_Strategy). Medway's stated aim is that both sides hold strong powers, not +1/-1 balance.
5. **Quiet roles cause disengagement.** Bumbling Through Dungeons names passive villager roles as the thing that makes players check out (https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/the-problem-with-social-deduction-board-games). Source-backed remedies: a trusted pair, partial information, a last shot.
6. **Small tables need slack.** Two wolves among five or six leave almost no margin; early death means no shared history; claims become proof (https://werewolv.es/small-setups/why-low-player-count-werewolf-stops-feeling-like-werewolf). Keep the uninformed majority above half and give it tools (minifiniti).
7. **Do not reveal the strong role early; make claims arguable.** A strong seer or protector distorts small games more than large ones (werewolv.es balance guidance, via search snippet: https://werewolv.es/how-to-balance-a-werewolf-game , unverified); see also the earlier notes on claiming Day 1.
8. **Make every player feel their hand in the result, and keep the win condition one sentence long** (minifiniti).
9. **Avoid early kills and arbitrary targeting of newcomers.** Ultimate Werewolf guidance as summarised by League of Gamemakers: no early kills, give the seer a night-zero view.
10. **Rule reminders help weak models.** WereBench found that restating the relevant rules helped small LLMs on speech tasks and that models play Witch, Werewolf and Seer best (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11389v1). Relevant here because adding toggles increases the risk that cheaper seats misread them: state each active toggle once, in the fixed system prompt.

## (d) Spectator-enjoyment evidence

Honest summary: **I found no study, designer talk or paper that tests whether viewers enjoy knowing the roles.** Everything below is soft.

- Among Us commentary credits social tension, lying and personalities clashing for watchability, with short rounds helping streams (search snippets of Wired, Screen Rant and fan guides; not verified at source). None addresses viewers knowing the impostor. (https://screenrant.com/twitch-most-popular-games-not-fun-among-us/ , https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/among-us-social-deduction-games-psychology)
- A public AI Mafia site presents games as readable transcripts; the reported draw is that bot failures (contradictions, forgetting claims, following the last speaker, over-explaining) are easy to see, and observers can follow accusations, defences, votes and night actions (https://geekchamp.com/ai-bots-now-play-mafia-with-each-other-on-public-website-and-almost-all-of-them-are-terrible-at-it/). Single article, not an audience study.
- Wolfcha, an open-source project with a "spectator mode" where viewers watch models compete (search snippet: https://github.com/oil-oil/wolfcha ), and a Reddit post describing a livestreamed AI Mafia game that restarts hourly (search snippet; unverified) show others building the same thing, but no engagement numbers.
- The Verbal Werewolf paper adds text-to-speech to boost engagement but reports no user study (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00160v1). It notes smaller models are repetitive and incoherent, and one model judged characters by the real-life reputations of the voices used.
- Game Wisdom argues some games are more fun to watch than play, and a BotC blog says watching a well-played game can match playing (search snippets, unverified: https://game-wisdom.com/general/games-fun-watch-play).
- Inference, not evidence: dramatic irony probably works because the audience can see the hidden killer lie in real time. The project's design implication is a presentation toggle, not a rule: show roles to spectators by default, keep a "guess along" mode that hides them until the end. Nothing here tests which mode viewers prefer.
- Medway frames BotC as built for a "maximum climax", with the thrill coming from solving the puzzle rather than reading faces (Behind the Curtain 4, above). A good fit for an AI table, where body language does not exist.
