# Real human Mafia / Werewolf table talk, second pass

Research note, 2026-11-14. Companion to `table-talk-samples.md` (corpora S1-S9) and `how-games-go.md`. This pass looks for **new** human-played records: open forum game threads, plus a re-check of the Greek sources. Only pages I actually opened are reported. Quotes are under 15 words. Anything marked **(fetch summary)** came through a summarising fetch, so the wording is not checked against the page; treat it as paraphrase-grade until someone opens the page.

## (a) Summary

1. Four new open human game threads were read (DnD Beyond 2023, Straight Dope 2015 and 2011, GOG Mafia #59 2019). They are slow, chatty, and full of meta-jokes about the game itself.
2. Day 1 in these threads is mostly thin accusations: "vote and find out", a "sus" post with no evidence, a self-vote, or a claim that someone is "acting really super sus".
3. Teasing is constant and personal (spelling, avatars, "interesting" vs "flopping"), and it is often the only reason someone gets targeted.
4. Defences are short and deflecting: "I think you're reading into it", "my ego can handle that", "I was just responding to questions".
5. Bandwagons form on a single post. One player votes, others copy with "vote X" or "wagon" within a few posts.
6. Role claims come early and are often thin ("I'm town", "I haven't played with them"), then get attacked for timing ("late claim, hastily put together").
7. Annoyance is expressed as "/groan", "feeling disrespected", or "irritating", and it lingers into the next day.
8. Moderators/hosts have their own voice: dry rule answers, wink-style asides, and quiet asides like "I'm sure you find this helpful."
9. Players sign off in a human way ("time for bed", "til morning all"), and claim tiredness or device trouble ("tags are a pain from an iPad").
10. None of the sources has a Greek Palermo transcript. The Greek search turned up app listings and listicles only. The one Greek video (S5) from the first pass remains the only Greek sample.

## (c) Patterns real humans show that an AI table usually lacks

1. **Day 1 accusations often have no reason.** Real players say "vote X" or "you're sus" with no evidence, then move on. The AI usually gives a reason every time.
2. **Teasing about irrelevant things.** Avatars, spelling, typing style, and "you're massively over-estimating your posts" appear with no game purpose. The AI tends to stay on the case.
3. **Jokes that break the game.** Lines like "chorus of the first Mafia-themed punk rock song" or "Ooh! Flub's changed his avatar!" are off-topic and never explained.
4. **Short defences.** "I'm just responding to questions", "my ego can handle that", "reading into it." Real defenders often do not argue well; they shrug.
5. **Bandwagons on one post.** "Vote X", "gogtrial wagon", "Dante G 3 (Prof P, Diggit, me)". A single copy-paste vote starts the pile-on; the AI tends to deliberate at length.
6. **Bare role claims.** "I'm town." Then the attack is on the timing of the claim ("late claim, hastily put together"), not its content.
7. **Claims get questioned for being thin or for "claiming for no reason".** A vigilante claim can be called "irritating" with no further proof.
8. **Non-answers are common.** "Maybe. Maybe not. I can't say." Real players dodge a direct question without penalty and let others push.
9. **Annoyance is shown, not described.** "/groan", "feeling disrespected", "C'mon, man!" Frustration is a word, not a paragraph.
10. **Hosts have a dry, slightly wry voice.** "Was intended. I want to see what happens without it." "I'm sure you find this helpful." Narrator lines are short and sometimes a joke at a player's expense.
11. **Rule clarifications are brief and final.** "Both die." The host does not explain at length.
12. **Tiredness, device trouble and off-topic life.** "Time for bed. Til morning all." "Goddamn tags are a pain from an iPad." Real players mention their situation.
13. **Players return to old accusations.** "I still think you're a wolf" is carried over across days, even when new evidence appears.
14. **Newcomers are teased, not protected.** "Now you have met ATPG he'll grow on you." The table welcomes a new player by teasing.
15. **Votes are announced with commentary.** Vote counts come with a jab ("Everyone else, step right up and don't be shy!"). The count is never just a number.
16. **Pointed quips about pairs.** "Like two peas in the pod, they are…" Real players notice voting pairs and say so briefly.
17. **Silence is noticed.** "Giraffe went quiet" is a real accusation, and the rebuttal is "I posted within hours of day's end". Players track each other's activity, not just content.
18. **Emotion carries over into the next day.** Annoyance from Day 1 comes back as "I still think you're a wolf" on Day 2.
19. **Sign-offs and small talk happen mid-game.** "Time for bed", "til morning all." The table has a social layer, not only a game layer.
20. **Some players refuse to engage with the logic at all.** "vote and find out. lol =D" is a real line of play: the vote is the test, not the argument.
